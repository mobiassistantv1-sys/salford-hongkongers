Safeguarding Policy

Salford Hongkongers CIC (Company No. 13877472) — Safeguarding Policy Statement

1. Purpose. Salford Hongkongers CIC runs community activities and the volunteer-run Yau Oi Cafe (友愛冰室) at The Angel Centre, Salford. We are committed to keeping children, young people and adults at risk safe from harm while they take part in our activities, visit the café or volunteer with us.

2. Scope. This policy applies to directors, volunteers, sessional helpers and anyone acting on behalf of the CIC, at all CIC activities, the café, online channels (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp channels, website) and partner events we run.

3. Context. This policy follows Working Together to Safeguard Children (Department for Education), the Care Act 2014 for adults at risk, and the local procedures of Salford's safeguarding partnerships. It links to the procedures listed in section 6.

4. We believe that:

  • Every child and adult has the right to be safe, whatever their age, disability, sex, race, religion or belief, sexual orientation, gender identity or immigration status.
  • Some people are more vulnerable, for example newly arrived families with limited English or support networks, older people, and people with disabilities.
  • Everyone involved with the CIC shares responsibility for reporting concerns.

5. We will keep people safe by:

  1. Appointing a Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL) and a deputy (section 7).
  2. Recruiting volunteers safely: application, reference, and an enhanced DBS check for volunteers who look after children under 18 at activities without their parents.
  3. Giving every volunteer a safeguarding induction before their first shift, refreshed every year.
  4. Never leaving a child under 18 alone with one adult who is not their parent or carer; children under 11 must be accompanied by a parent or carer at all activities.
  5. Getting consent before taking or posting photos of identifiable people; never posting photos of children without a parent's written consent.
  6. Keeping safeguarding records confidential, factual and secure, shared only with the DSL and statutory agencies when needed (UK GDPR).
  7. Using our whistleblowing route (section 7) when a concern is about a director or the DSL.

6. Procedures that sit under this policy: responding to a concern or disclosure; volunteer recruitment and DBS; code of conduct for volunteers; photography and social media; online safety (below); complaints and whistleblowing; Privacy Policy.

7. If you are worried:

  • Immediate danger: call 999.
  • Tell the DSL the same day: Manson, 07999 338006, mansonyiu@salfordhongkongers.co.uk. Deputy: Evelyn, hkerssalford@gmail.com.
  • Concern about the DSL or a director: tell the deputy DSL (Evelyn), or report directly to Salford social care using the numbers below.
  • Child: Salford Bridge Partnership 0161 603 4500 (8.30am–4.30pm). Adult at risk: Salford Adult Social Care 0808 504 0350 (Mon–Fri 8.30am–4.30pm). Out of hours, child or adult: Emergency Duty Team 0161 794 8888.
  • Advice: NSPCC Helpline 0808 800 5000; Childline 0800 1111.

8. Review. This policy comes into force on 24 November 2026 and is reviewed every year, or sooner after a serious incident or a change in law. Next review: 24 November 2027.

9. Signed: Manson, Director, 24 November 2026.

Health & Safety Policy

Part 1 — Statement of intent. We will take reasonable care to protect the health and safety of volunteers, visitors, café customers and anyone affected by our activities. We have the same duty of care to volunteers as to paid staff. We will assess risks, give people the information and training they need, and review this policy every year.

Part 2 — Responsibilities

RoleResponsible for
Directors (overall: Manson)This policy, insurance, annual review, acting on reported hazards
Café lead on shiftOpening and closing checks, food safety records, briefing volunteers on the day
Event lead (named for each event)Event risk assessment, first aid cover, emergency briefing
Every volunteerFollowing training and instructions, reporting hazards, accidents and near misses

Part 3 — Arrangements

  1. Risk assessment. Written risk assessments for the café and for each event, using HSE's five steps; reviewed every year or after an incident or change.
  2. Food safety (Yau Oi Cafe). Registered food business with Salford City Council; food safety management using the FSA's Safer Food, Better Business pack; temperature and cleaning records each trading day; allergen information for every menu item (UK 14 allergens) on the menu; at least 2 volunteers on each shift with Level 2 Food Hygiene.
  3. Training and induction. Every new volunteer is shown this policy, fire exits, first aid kit location, safe use of kitchen equipment, manual handling and allergen procedure before their first shift.
  4. First aid. First aid kit in the kitchen floor cupboard; one volunteer with basic first aid knowledge acts as the appointed person on each shift (takes charge of first aid arrangements and calls 999; gives only the first aid they are trained for); qualified first aiders are arranged when an event's risk assessment needs them; accidents are written in the accident book within 24 hours.
  5. Fire and emergencies. Follow The Angel Centre's fire procedure; assembly points are outside the rear metal gate and outside the refuse collection point door; the shift lead takes a headcount of volunteers.
  6. Equipment and premises. Electrical items are visually checked before use and PAT-tested where our agreement with The Angel Centre requires it; faults are reported to The Angel Centre and taken out of use.
  7. Lone working. At least two volunteers open and close the café; no one does it alone.
  8. Insurance. Insured with Unity, renewed each May; cover includes our activities, the café, volunteers, participants and the public.
  9. Reporting. If a work-related incident (for example in the café kitchen) causes a death, or someone is taken straight from the scene to hospital for treatment, a director agrees with The Angel Centre (in control of the premises) who reports it to HSE under RIDDOR. Precautionary hospital visits are not reportable.

Review. In force from 24 November 2026; next review 24 November 2027. Signed: Manson, Director, 24 November 2026.

Online Safety Procedure

This procedure sits under the Safeguarding Policy. It covers our website, Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp channels and groups, and any online event we run.

1. Risks we watch for (NSPCC's 4 Cs): harmful content; unwanted contact such as grooming or scam approaches; harmful conduct such as bullying or sharing private images; and commerce such as scams and phishing aimed at Hongkongers.

2. Accounts

  1. CIC and café accounts are run only by volunteers the directors approve; logins are not shared outside them and use two-step verification.
  2. Volunteers contact participants through CIC accounts, groups or channels, never personal accounts.
  3. Volunteers do not accept friend or follow requests from children under 18 they meet through the CIC, and do not message them privately.

3. Groups and channels

  1. Each group has at least two admins.
  2. House rules are posted in each group: be respectful, no hate speech, no adverts or scams, no sharing others' private details or photos.
  3. Admins remove posts that break the rules and keep a screenshot as a record; repeat or serious cases are referred to the DSL.
  4. Under-18s join a CIC group only with a parent or carer's consent.

4. Photos and personal data online. Follow the Photography and Social Media procedure: consent before posting an identifiable person; written parent consent for any child; never post names, school or home details of a child.

5. If something worrying happens online

  1. Immediate danger: call 999.
  2. Do not delete the evidence: screenshot it with the date, time and account name.
  3. Tell the DSL (Manson) or deputy (Evelyn) the same day.
  4. Report the account to the platform; the DSL decides whether to refer to Salford social care or the police.
  5. A child who tells you something online: never promise to keep it secret; point them to Childline 0800 1111.
  6. Scams or attacks on CIC accounts are also recorded as evidence and reported to the platform and, where needed, the police.

6. Review. Reviewed with the Safeguarding Policy every year. In force from 24 November 2026; next review 24 November 2027. Signed: Manson, Director, 24 November 2026.
